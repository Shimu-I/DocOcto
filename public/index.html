About the project

The problem

Writers of research papers need a careful reviewer. General grammar tools rewrite freely, miss citation mistakes, upload your text to the cloud, and cannot work on a PDF as it is. A wrong edit to a number or a citation can quietly harm a paper.

What DocOcto does

It opens your PDF or Word file, finds writing problems with several free checkers, and marks each one on the exact words with a reason and a suggested fix. Suggestions that would change numbers, citations or equations, or make a claim stronger, are removed before you see them. You accept or reject each one.

Its purpose is simple: help you polish your writing while keeping full control of your work.

Features

What you can do with DocOcto.

Real pages

PDFs appear as the real pages and Word files keep their formatting. Problems are marked on the exact words.

Four checks

Quick rules, grammar (LanguageTool), AI (free or local) and APA citation consistency. All optional and free.

Safe suggestions

Every suggestion is checked in code. Anything that changes a number, a citation or an equation, or makes a claim stronger, is discarded.

You stay in control

Fix, reject or undo each change. A history and a summary show exactly what changed.

Private by default

Your text stays on your computer. A cloud AI is optional and always asks first.

Export

Download the revised text as Word or Markdown, plus a review report.

Easy to read

Large text, a text-size button, calm colours, keyboard shortcuts and reduced-motion support.

Octopus guide

An animated octopus points at the problem you are looking at. Soften, pause or hide it at any time.

    User guide

    Everything you need to use DocOcto, in plain language.

    This is the full local edition. Grammar check, the citation check and Word export are available.

    1. Getting started

    1. On the Home page, drop a paper into Start reviewing, or press Open app (top right) and then Upload paper. You can also press Try the demo paper.
    2. The paper opens on the Review screen and Quick check runs by itself.
    3. Click an issue in the list on the right. Read the reason, then press Fix or Reject.
    4. When you finish, open Summary and download your revised text.

    2. Supported files and limits

    FormatSupport
    PDF with selectable textYes, shown as real pages (up to 400 pages)
    Word .docxYes, with formatting
    Text and MarkdownYes
    Scanned PDFNot yet. Run it through OCR first
    Old .doc, .odt, LaTeX, slides, spreadsheetsNo. Convert to .docx, or upload the PDF
    • Maximum 20 MB, 20,000 paragraphs and 400 PDF pages. Password-protected PDFs do not open.
    • English only.
    • Good input: a paper with real text, a heading called References for the citation check, and author-year (APA) citations such as (Smith, 2019).

    3. The review screen

    • Top bar: file name, View/Edit, the checks, export buttons, Summary and Settings.
    • Document (middle): your paper with issues marked. Thumbnails jump to a page. The bar underneath has zoom, tools and display switches.
    • Right panel: issue counts by type, the list of every issue (with a Fix button), the selected suggestion, and your change history.

    4. Colours and underlines

    Each type of problem has its own colour and underline style, so you never rely on colour alone.

    TypeUnderline
    GrammarWavy underline
    ClaritySolid underline
    Style & toneDotted underline
    RepetitionDashed underline
    CitationDouble underline
    StructureLine above and below

    Suggestions are also labelled: Likely error (an objective mistake), Optional style (a preference), Citation check (your citations against your reference list) and Review only (nothing to replace, such as a very long sentence; hidden until you press its button).

    5. Reviewing suggestions

    • Fix / Accept applies the suggestion. Reject keeps your words. Undo reverses the last action.
    • Items with no replacement offer Mark reviewed and Dismiss.
    • The preview shows the old text struck through and the new text in green.
    • Nothing is changed without your click, and your original file is never modified.

    6. Reading the results

    • Each suggestion shows the original text, the proposed change and a short reason. Read the reason before accepting.
    • Likely error means a clear mistake such as a repeated word. Optional style is a preference you can ignore.
    • The Checks run line says which checks ran, which failed, and how many suggestions were discarded as unsafe.
    • A count of zero does not mean the paper is perfect. DocOcto cannot judge meaning, logic or science, and it never checks that a cited source exists.
    • Summary counts what you accepted, rejected and left open, and downloads a report.

    7. The checks

    • Quick check: built-in rules for common mistakes. Needs nothing. Runs on upload.
    • Grammar check: LanguageTool for grammar and spelling. Local edition. Needs Java: sudo apt install -y openjdk-17-jre-headless then pip install -r requirements-grammar.txt.
    • AI check: an AI reviews the text. Use Ollama on your computer, or a free Gemini or Groq key in the .env file. A cloud provider receives your text only after you confirm.
    • Citation check: compares author-year (APA) citations with your reference list: missing references, wrong years, ambiguous citations, author-count mismatches and references never cited.

    8. PDF and Word files

    • PDF pages are shown as they are. Highlights are thin underlines; the selected one is filled. If text cannot be found on the page, the issue stays in the list only.
    • A PDF is never edited. Press Edit to change the revised text; press View to return to the pages. Accept fixes first, then edit.
    • Word files keep headings, bold and italic, lists, tables and images. Accepted fixes change the text in place.
    • Scanned PDFs need OCR first: ocrmypdf scan.pdf readable.pdf.
    • Zoom keeps your place. Page: soft / dark / original changes PDF page colour.

    9. Exporting

    • Word (.docx) and Markdown contain your revised text (headings, paragraphs and bullets). Bold, tables, images and layout are not carried over.
    • The review report (from Summary) lists your changes and history.

    11. Settings

    • Text size (top bar or Settings): Normal, Large or Extra large. Your choice is remembered.
    • Octopus: bold or soft, on or off, and Pause. It also respects your system's reduce-motion setting.
    • Jump: when on, selecting an issue scrolls the document to it. When off (default) the page only moves when you scroll; use the magnifier button to jump.

    12. Example of correct use

    1. Press Try the demo paper. The sentence “the proposed method is very good” is underlined in yellow.
    2. Click it in the list. The card says “Very good” is vague and suggests strong.
    3. Press Fix. The text changes, and the Change history records it. Press Undo to restore it.
    4. Open Summary to see the count and download the report.

    13. Problems and answers

    My upload was rejected.

    Check the file type (PDF, .docx, .txt or .md) and size (20 MB at most). A message under the drop area or at the bottom of the screen says what is wrong.

    It says no selectable text was found.

    The PDF is a scan. Run it through OCR first.

    Many suggestions look irrelevant.

    Hide Optional style or Review only with their buttons, and reject freely. Add technical words to ignore_words.txt for the grammar check.

    The page view does not load.

    The viewers need two small libraries downloaded once. Run python scripts/get_vendor.py with internet. Until then the text is shown as plain paragraphs.

    Is my paper sent anywhere?

    Not unless you choose a cloud AI and confirm. Everything else runs on your computer.

    Can it fix my PDF directly?

    No. It keeps your PDF untouched and gives you a revised text to export.

    About and technical details

    How DocOcto works, how well it works, and where it falls short.

    Methodology

    1. Extraction. The browser reads the PDF (PDF.js) or Word file (Mammoth) and splits it into paragraphs. The original file is not changed.
    2. Checks. Up to four checkers propose suggestions: built-in rules, LanguageTool, an AI model, and an APA citation consistency check.
    3. Validation. One validator filters every suggestion. It must be locatable, must not overlap another, and must not change a number, add a citation, alter an equation or strengthen a claim.
    4. Review. Each remaining suggestion is anchored by paragraph, exact phrase and occurrence number, drawn on the page, and applied only if you accept it.

    Preprocessing

    • PDF text lines are grouped into paragraphs; numbered headings are separated from body text.
    • Repeated running headers, footers and page numbers are removed.
    • Tables, headings and non-prose text are skipped by the style rules; sentences over 150 words are treated as extraction errors.

    Data and evaluation

    The built-in rules were scored on a small set of 35 labelled issues in 41 short paragraphs (8 contain no issue). Matching is strict: a suggestion counts only if its text overlaps the labelled text by at least half.

    MeasureBuilt-in rules
    Precision82%
    Recall51%
    F163%
    Lenient precision / recall86% / 54%
    False alarms per 1,000 words10
    Clean paragraphs wrongly flagged12%
    Correct category / fix among matches94% / 89%
    Read this first. The labels were written by an AI assistant, not independent people, and the set partly matches what the rules were built to find. Real papers will score lower. Recall by type: citations 100%, style 100%, structure 100%, clarity 71%, repetition 50%, grammar 12%. LanguageTool and AI have not been scored yet.

    Known limitations

    • Less accurate than paid tools. Rules miss most grammar-agreement errors.
    • PDFs are never edited; fixes go into a separate revised text. Scanned PDFs need OCR. Two-column PDFs can be read out of order.
    • English and APA author-year citations only. It never checks that a source exists or supports a claim.
    • It cannot judge meaning, logic or scientific correctness.
